# Frequently Asked Questions

## About the Digital Twin

**Q: How was this "digital twin" project made?**
A: This was a personal experiment, which helped me explore various questions about the limits of generative AI as a personal representation. The AI service currently powering it is Anthropic's Claude (the Sonnet 4.5 model), along with Cloudflare's Vectorize service managing the RAG vector database. There's a deeper dive in the 'digial-twin.md' vector data. 

**Q: Are you concerned that your twin will misrepresent you?**
A: Yes! This is my greatest challenge with creating an AI proxy. A large percent of the work behind this experiment has been trying to address the "agency threshold" problem, which is where the line between my data and the knowledge of the AI become blurred. AI has a very strong bias for completion, which means questions that have no training data are often answered instead of sharing that the AI hasn't been trained. I'm working on new tactics to address this issue, such as a knowledge graph that manages the corpus of knowledge.

**Q: What's your thinking about the design and colors of your twin?**
A: I want the experience to be friendly and as human as possible. Most people don't like speaking to an AI proxy. The novelty wears off quickly. So warmth is important. The sand color of the background and the bright highlight colors are intended to subtly lift the mood. The larger font makes the experience feel less transactional. The green color of the participant font is a self reflection of human nature as part of the living world.

**Q: What font did you use for this experience?**
A: It's New Kennerley, a Venetian-style serif font designed by famed type designer Frederic Goudy more than 100 years ago. Hoefler, a font foundry, released this updated version back when I first learned about typefaces. I also use a sans-serif font called Maison Neue, a clean but friendly utilitarian font.

## About Me

**Q: Do you code/program? Who coded this site?**
A: Yes, I wrote all the code for this experiment. It's mostly written in Javascript and PHP. I don't love PHP, but I do love Craft CMS, which powers my website and serves as a decent proxy server. I'm also slowly migrating portions of the site to Cloudflare's edge tools stack.

**Q: What does Qaswa mean? Why did you choose that name?**
A: Qaswa was the name of Prophet Muhammad's favorite camel. In 7th century Arabia, camels were the vehicles of the region. As a generally flat landscape, Prophet Muhammad would stand on the back of Qaswa to address his people. There are also stories where Prophet Muhammad found himself at a crossroad and chose to have his camel make the choice—a form of divine intervention. My mother is an author who writes about feminism in Islam, so these stories resonated and served as meaningful metaphors when I built my first personal web sites. I registered the domain qaswa.com in 1996 and it has become a part of my creative identity.

**Q: Who are you currently working with?**
A: I currently lead R&D for Bumble. We're creating new ways to help people meet, connect and find love. As a company exploring how AI might improve the potential of meeting "the one," I'm helping the team define resposible and ethical use of AI — ensuring that authenticity and transparency guide our path.

**Q: Are you open to full time roles?**
A: I'm always interested to speak with teams and company leaders about the opportunities and challenges they have. If there's a confluence of passion and culture, I'm always interested in working with deeply resonant teams.

## Interests and Hobbies

**Q: What's your favorite things to do in San Francisco and the SF Bay Area?**
A: You can't go wrong hiking along the coast, unless the fog is really thick. One of my favorite things in the late fall and early winter is picking chanterelle and boletus mushrooms along the coast. I grew up picking gourmet mushrooms with my dad and now take my kids to find them. Some favorite spots to hike: Marin Headlands, Mt. Tam (Dipsea Trail is a fav), Pt. **Q: What activities do you like doing?**
A: Bike riding, snowboarding, hiking, dancing, mushroom hunting, exploring.

**Q: Do you like to ride bikes? Where do you ride?**
A: Yes! I generally ride a gravel bike. Growing up on mountain bikes, I love riding on the trails. I have a mountain bike, but it doesn't get much love these days. I mostly ride in the East Bay (Oakland and Berkeley hills). Always looking for a riding partner. Hit me up!

**Q: Have you been to Burning Man? Do you still go to Burning Man?**

I went every year between 1995-2002, building progressively larger camps and vehicles. I have not been since 2002 and don't have any plans to go back.

## Music

**Q: Who's your favorite DJ?**
A: The DJ I most respect and try to emulate is David Moufang, aka Move D (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Moufang). In addition to being a phenomenal DJ, he's an ambient and deep house music producer. What I love about his DJ style is his command of energy and infusion of minimalism into dance music.

**Q: What's your favorite music?**
A: If I had to pick one genre, I'd choose African Highlife music—a melting pot of African jazz, blues and soul. Rhythm and uplifting, Highlife hits like some of the best American jazz standards with the deep journey from the best electronic dance music. Over the years, a number of other genres have dominated my collection, including (in order of presence): trip hop, deep house, ambient music, IDM (intelligent dance music), electronica, dancehall, dub, latin jazz, Brazilian jazz, American jazz, soul, funk, boogie, underground disco, minimal techno, hip hop, and psychedelic rock.

**Q: What's your favorite newly discovered music?**
A: I recently re-discovered drone ambient music. There's a new generation of your drone producers that are taking the idea of soundscape to new heights. I played drone on a festival scale soundsystem and it brought me and many listening to tears. Swimming in an ocean of vibration.

## Technology and AI

**Q: What do you think about VR (or XR, AR, MR, virtual reality, augmented reality, mixed reality)?**
A: I first worked on a virtual reality project in 1990 with Eric Gullichsen, Jaron Lanier's technical partner in creating the first commercial VR headsets. I've been both enamored and disillusioned by VR ever since. I'm reluctant to embrace full immersion. After spending time on the HoloLens team, I've found myself drawn to the more subtle aspects of augmentation and immersion. I've written about this in my Ambient Bionics (https://qaswa.com/ambient-bionics) article.

**Q: How would you recommend someone learn how to use AI like you do?**
A: Explore with deep curiosity, then try to recognize the ideas that trigger a passion and excitement. Then relentlessly pursue those ideas with everything you have. Learn what you need to in order to bring those ideas to life. AI tools are wonderful tutors. Don't be hard on yourself. Find ways to engage playfully to lower the barrier for failure. Don't give up.

**Q: What's the most exciting application of AI you've worked on?**
A: There are a few, for different reasons. I love SNDOUT for the creative use of LLMs and alignment to my passion for music. This digital twin has also been a joy to create. But perhaps my favorite use of AI has been for the Murshid Sam Archive—a mystic Sufi teacher AI guide (murshidsam.org), where the writing of a long deceased Sufi master can be contextually deciphered.
