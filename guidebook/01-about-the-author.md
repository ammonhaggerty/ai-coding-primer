# About the Author

I've been building things on and off screens for thirty years. My first computer was an Apple II+ with a 300-baud modem. I was twelve, and what hooked me wasn't the technology — it was that I could make things appear out of nothing. Procedural line drawings in BASIC. I'd write a few lines of code and watch the screen fill with patterns I hadn't fully imagined. That feeling — imagining something and watching it come to life — has been the through-line of my entire career.

I spent most of my career as a "creative technologist" - a hybrid designer/developer. I honed my craft at a boutique agency called Odopod, where we help Nike launch their SB (Skateboarding) brand. I expanded into physical and experiential work at a company called Obscura, where we built large scale immersive environments - dipping my toes into hardware. That lead to working on the early Hololens team and AI projects at Microsoft. This is all to say that I've seen a broad range of teams solving really hard problems with the highest level of craft and creativity. 

I've also been a founder three times. At Formation, we built an AI-powered loyalty platform that delivered over a billion dollars in incremental value to Starbucks alone — raised $30 million, grew to 140 people, got acquired by BCG. At Joinable, we built a solution for the loneliness epidemic with tools for in-person connection. Currently, I lead AI R&D and new product incubation at Bumble, where we're exploring how AI can contribute to people feeling safe and empowered, while avoiding the side of AI that steals personal agency and authenticity. 

I've spent my career living in the space between "what if we built this?" and "how would we actually build it?" — and for most of that time, both halves required me. The imagining and the making. The design and the code.

What's changed is that the making part has a partner now.

My path to AI-assisted building has been a gradual migration. Stack Overflow was my first real crutch — search for a problem, find a solution, adapt it. GitHub Copilot felt like autocomplete that occasionally read my mind. ChatGPT was useful for explanations but couldn't touch my actual files. Cursor wrapped AI into the editor, and that's where things started to accelerate — I could describe what I wanted and watch code appear in context.

Claude Code was a step change. I cancelled my Cursor subscription the day it was officially released.

I wrote an article last year called '80/20 - AI's Predictable Unpredictability' about the persistent 20% failure rate that has plagued AI tools and services. The article resonated with many friends and colleagues deep in the trenches of AI product development. Claude Code made this feel more manageable - likely to do with the collaborative framing within a more direct development context. But something changed with the recent Opus releases - by Opus 4.8 the 20% failures went away almost entirely. This coincied with rapid enhancements to Claude Code, including MCP servers for code knowledge, Claude Skills, and sub-agents that could specialize. All together, the landscape changed overnight and I want from writing and reviewing code to simply orchestrating.

I built an [iOS app](https://github.com/ammonhaggerty/anaspace) over a weekend. Not a tutorial project — a real app. Every feature generated by Claude. Every bug diagnosed and fixed by Claude. Every App Store configuration guided by Claude. I never opened the code in an editor to make a manual change. That had never happened before in my career.

One more piece of context: I spent the past few years building with React, Next.JS and Vercel — the tech stack used by many of my friends and colleagues. It's an excellent stack and Vercel makes a great product. But I wanted something looser — less build pipeline, less configuration glue, more room to move fast on scrappy experiments. I've been using HTMX in personal projects for years — lightweight, runtime, powerful — and it pairs naturally with Cloudflare. Workers, R2, D1, AI, DNS, all under one account. When I made the switch, it felt like a weight lifted. The whole stack is integrated in a way that makes AI-assisted development dramatically smoother, because there are fewer seams for things to go wrong. I know it won't meet the needs for eveyone, but for anyone jumping in with no coding skills, I believe this will get you to where you want faster and with less pain. 

This article comes from that experience. Not from a weekend experiment or a thought exercise — from months of building and shipping real prototypes, weekly, at a company where the work matters. I wrote this because the path I found is open to anyone with imagination and curiosity, and almost nobody knows it exists yet.
